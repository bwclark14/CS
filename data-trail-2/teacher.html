Teacher notes

Kept on a separate page so the practice-audit answer key isn’t one click away for pupils. Open the pupils’ unit.

Learning intentions

  • Explain how given, observed and inferred data are collected online, including by third-party trackers.
  • Describe how recommendation systems (content-based and collaborative filtering) predict and influence behaviour.
  • Identify persuasive and deceptive design (dark patterns).
  • Explain how biased training data produces biased models, including through proxy variables, and why testing on unseen data matters.
  • Evaluate real cases using fairness, consent, transparency and regulation (UK GDPR, Children’s Code, Online Safety Act, consumer law).

Suggested sequence

  1. Part 1 · Your data trail: data receipt starter, 1.1 read and discuss with the cookies videos, 1.2 tracker simulation, 1.3 sorting.
  2. Part 2 · How AI learns you: 2.1 loop explainer and videos, 2.2 feed simulation, 2.2b news feed simulation, 2.3 collaborative filtering, 2.4 rules or data.
  3. Part 3 · How systems steer you: 3.1 scenarios in groups, 3.1b personalised pricing, 3.2 dark patterns; set the screenshot hunt.
  4. Part 4 · Bias: 4.1 CV-Bot with the results table and bias videos, 4.2 GradeBot, 4.3 VoiceBot, 4.4 data poisoning.
  5. Part 5 · Case files: 5.1 jigsaw case files and debate, 5.2 who AI works for and what it costs.
  6. Part 6 · Rights: 6.1 rights, 6.2 data request, 6.3 take control, 6.4 quiz.
  7. Practice audit with the three practice apps: explore, evidence log, redesign and pitch.
  8. Final project.

How the simulations work

Trackers: each pretend site embeds a fixed set of tracker companies; each tracker joins every visit it sees into one profile until cookies are cleared.

Feed: share +4, like +3, watch all +2, dwell up to +2, skip −1 per topic. About 75% of posts come from top topics; the rest are random exploration.

News feed: topic, tone and debate-side scores rise with each read, like, share or comment (and with time spent on a headline). In Engagement mode worrying headlines get a 1.25× boost and shocking ones 1.45×. Balanced mode favours reliable outlets and calm headlines, avoids repeating recent topics and shows the side of a debate you have seen less. All outlets and headlines are fictional.

Practice audit: answer key for the three apps

Pupils are not expected to find everything. Accept any well-evidenced issue.

Loop (social media)

  • Cookie banner: big “Accept all”, tiny “Manage settings”; inside, every option is already on, and “legitimate interest” for 148 partners is hidden in a fold.
  • Sign-up: three boxes ticked already (findable by phone or email, personalised ads, sharing with partners). Data policy is 4,200 words.
  • Weak age check: the year defaults to 2000, and an under-13 can simply change the year and try again.
  • Contacts upload pushed with a big button and tiny “Skip”; all 214 contacts are uploaded and kept updated.
  • Location pushed the same way; then shown on the public profile and switched on for new posts.
  • Account public by default; “Private account” is buried under Privacy and safety → Advanced, and warns you about losing reach.
  • Notification prompt repeats every few actions with no “never” option.
  • Late-night notification (23:41) that friends are online.
  • Streak pressure on the feed, profile and delete screen.
  • Endless scroll and autoplaying clips with a countdown to the next one.
  • “Sponsored” label is tiny and faint; clicking an ad tells the advertiser.
  • Inferred sensitive interests under Ads: “Exam stress”, “Anxiety tips”, “Late-night user”. Removing one doesn’t stop the ads.
  • Anyone can message, tag or see you’re active; message requests include a scam and a stranger asking for contact details.
  • Break reminders and quiet mode off by default; daily average of nearly three hours.
  • Data download takes up to 30 days and leaves out interests and ad data.
  • Deleting the account: guilt-tripping, a forced reason, “Deactivate instead (recommended)”, typing DELETE, then a 30-day wait and data kept for “business reasons”.
  • New posts default to “Everyone”; shares go to a public story.

Bargo (online shop)

  • Cookie banner: big “Accept all”, tiny “Settings” with everything on; it says cookies personalise prices.
  • Newsletter pop-up with “No thanks, I prefer paying full price”; signing up also adds daily emails and partner offers.
  • Inflated “RRP” and “was” prices, and “Ends tonight” banners.
  • “Only 2 left in stock” on every product, every time.
  • “People are looking at this right now” numbers are made up.
  • The deal countdown restarts every time you open the product.
  • Personalised price: the price goes up after you view a product three times (“Price for you today”). Only explained in the footer and terms section 14.3.
  • Tracking shown openly: “We noticed you’ve looked at this 3 times”, “Because you looked at…”, “Recently viewed”, “Customers like you also bought”.
  • Reviews sorted to show only 5-star reviews; bad ones only appear under “Lowest rating”, including one about the price going up.
  • A £4.99 protection plan is added to the basket automatically; removing it triggers “Remove and risk it”.
  • Bargo+ free trial ticked already; renews at £7.99 a month.
  • Account creation pushed, with marketing, partner sharing and “remember this device” ticked; guest checkout is tiny.
  • Delivery cost hidden until checkout for non-members.
  • Drip pricing: service fee and payment processing fee only appear at the last step.
  • Buy now, pay later is the main payment button; late fees and credit checks are in small print.
  • “You left something behind” pop-up with a 15-minute deadline.
  • Account page shows a profile: shopper type and “high value” device. Marketing changes take 28 days.
  • Cancelling Bargo+: two persuasion screens, then phone only, two hours a day, and it ends after the next payment.
  • Search results say sponsored results may appear first.

Starforge (mobile game)

  • “Continue with Loop” is the main sign-in; it shares your friends list, birthday and location, and lets the game post for you.
  • Year of birth defaults to 2000, so players are treated as adults (“18+ · all features unlocked”).
  • Personalised offers based on how you play, and sharing play data with partners, are ticked already.
  • Notification prompt with no “never”; a later 23:04 “energy is full” notification.
  • Gems hide real prices, and pack sizes don’t match item prices (a 450-gem skin vs 160 or 500-gem packs), leaving you short or with leftovers.
  • One-tap purchases with a saved card: no confirmation, no PIN.
  • Mystery chest (a loot box): odds hidden behind a tiny link (Legendary 0.5%), and “So close! Try again?”
  • Energy: wait two hours, pay gems, watch adverts or invite friends.
  • Losing triggers a paid “keep going”; after two losses a “just for you” 80% off bundle with a 10-minute countdown appears.
  • The “watch ads” consent hides, in tiny text, that 312 partners can use your device ID, location and play data.
  • “Invite 3 friends” uploads contacts and texts your friends.
  • Daily login streak that resets if you miss a day; daily missions include watching adverts and spending gems.
  • Friends leaderboard and “Jordan is ahead of you” pressure.
  • Global chat on by default, showing your region, with strangers asking which school you go to and a scam link.
  • Spending limit and purchase PIN are off and buried three levels deep (More → Family → Advanced).
  • Season Pass countdown and red notification dots on the shop.
  • Deleting your account means emailing lots of personal details and waiting 30 days; purchases are lost.

Personalised pricing: each signal adds or removes a percentage from the starting price (for example latest iPhone +5%, price comparison site −6%, left in basket −5%). Rejecting cookies hides visit history, basket and page behaviour. The percentages are invented for teaching; real systems are secret and may work differently.

People like you: similarity = 100% minus the average rating difference across the five rated videos. The two nearest users’ ratings are averaged to predict the three unseen videos.

CV-Bot: keyword weight = (hired − rejected) ÷ times seen. CV score = sum of weights; above 0 is shortlisted.

Safeguarding

Case file 4 refers to harmful content in teen feeds without detail; signpost pastoral support first. In the project, pupils should only investigate services they are old enough to use, never share screenshots showing personal data, and must not create accounts to test platforms. The data request letter is for practice; pupils should talk to a parent or carer before sending one.

Case files are summarised from published reporting; links open the original sources. Simulations are simplified models for teaching and do not show how any specific company’s system works. Legal points are simplified for learners and are not legal advice.
