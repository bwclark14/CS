4.4 · Patterns

Why do predictions go wrong?

In 1978, Roy Amara noticed something that became known as Amara's Law: we usually overestimate what a new technology will do in the short run, and underestimate what it will do in the long run.

Right gadget, wrong date

Shopping and banking from home worked on Prestel in 1979. Hardly anyone used it. It took the web, 20 years later, to make it normal.

Gadgets, not habits

People imagined robot maids and glowing ceilings. Hardly anyone predicted we'd use phones as cameras, wallets and maps, or work from home.

Judging by today's limits

Experts said the internet would collapse and the iPhone would flop, because they judged them by what existed then.

Trends beat dreams

Moore measured a real trend in chips and was right for decades. Flying cars came from imagination and are still waiting.

Good stories spread

Made-up quotes survive because they are funny, not because they are true.

5.1 · Study the style

How 1980s computer adverts worked

Home computer adverts in UK magazines usually shouted the price, bragged about memory in "K" and made big claims. Many were sold by post: you cut out a coupon and waited weeks.

The 80s advert checklist

  • A big headline, often with the price
  • Specs as boasts: "48K!", "16 colours!"
  • A huge claim ("the world's best… for under £500")
  • The product on a plain black or white background
  • Mail order: "allow 28 days for delivery"
  • A pun, slogan or jingle line
  • Only words people knew in 1985

Sinclair's "28 days delivery" promise got it into trouble with the Advertising Standards Authority: some buyers waited 8–12 weeks.

    5.3 · Think like a critic

    Is your advert fair?

    Who is it talking to?

    Many 1980s computer adverts showed mostly boys and men. Some people think this helped put girls off computing. Who would feel included by your advert? Who might feel left out?

    Is every claim true?

    Today the Advertising Standards Authority checks that UK adverts are honest. Is there anything in your advert they might question?
