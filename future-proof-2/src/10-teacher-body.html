TEACHER NOTES

Future Proof 2: computing systems, then, now and next

For learners aged 11–14 (S1–S3), Third Level with Fourth Level extensions. Built on the draft Computing Science Know–Do–Understand framework (September 2026 edit). Not linked from the pupils' page.

At a glance

12 missions

Then (1–5), Now (6–8), Next (9–12). Each mission is its own page and ends at a natural stopping point with three "I can" statements pupils tick.

About 14–18 lessons

Missions 7 and 11 take 2–3 lessons each. Mission 1.3 (fetch–decode–execute) is optional: the Third Level framework excludes deep component-level hardware design.

No logins, no server

Pupil work is saved in the browser only (localStorage, keys starting fp2:). Shared computers: pupils should download what they need, or use "Start again on this device" in the Chrono Pass.

Chrono Pass

19 stamps, each earned by doing something (e.g. delivering a message after a cable cut, getting an ACK), not by visiting. No streaks, no leaderboards: this follows the ICO Children's Code and research on adolescents' status sensitivity.

Equipment

micro:bit V2s: ideally three per group. Missions 7.3 steps 6–8 and 7.4 need real hardware. Everything else runs in the browser.

Codenames

Pupils choose a codename on the briefing page. It labels their time capsule so you can match files to pupils without storing names.

AI and safeguarding

Scotland's AI in schools: guidelines and guardrails (25 March 2026, non-statutory) asks for local-authority approval of specific tools, critical (not uncritical) use, data protection and teacher oversight. It names no approved pupil tools.

Tier 1 · everyone

No-account tools: micro:bit CreateAI and MakeCode; Teachable Machine (Google says it trains on the device). The 7.5 bias experiment and 11.2 AI-model route use these.

Tier 2 · whole class

You run an authority-approved generative AI tool on the board, typing the class's prompts. Pupils critique outputs in the 11.3 evidence log.

Tier 3 · 13+ only

Only tools your authority has approved for pupils in an education account (e.g. Copilot Chat for students 13+, where enabled). Never personal consumer accounts.

Age terms as reported on 25 October 2026 (single secondary source: confirm on the providers' pages and with your authority): ChatGPT 13+ with parental permission under 18; Gemini apps not offered on supervised UK accounts; Claude consumer accounts 18+; Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat available to students 13+. The site never links pupils to a chatbot. Pupils who object to using AI can sketch two versions by hand instead (11.3).

Sensitive content

  • The cyber file mentions arrests of 17–20 year olds in the 2025 retail attacks without naming anyone. Frame it as "the same skills can go either way" and point to CyberFirst.
  • Deliberately left out for this age group: the 2025 Kido nursery data theft, deepfake "nudification" apps, and statistics about children using chatbots because they have no one else to talk to. Use your judgement if pupils raise them.
  • The brain–computer interface file is framed medically. Some pupils may have relatives with ALS or paralysis.

micro:bit notes

  • Every snippet uses MakeCode JavaScript. Pupils paste it in, then switch to Blocks. Test each snippet in MakeCode before the lesson: they were written for this unit and haven't been tested on hardware.
  • For two different programs in the simulator, use the multi-editor: makecode.microbit.org/---multi.
  • The browser simulator doesn't model transmit power, distance or signal strength. Steps 6–8 of the ladder and the 7.4 experiment need real boards.
  • Radio facts used in the unit: groups 0–255; strings up to 19 characters; no addressing, no encryption, no mesh; about 20 m at default power and up to about 70 m in open space at power 7.
  • The in-page three-board simulator is a teaching model, not a micro:bit emulator. It shows the logic of each level.

Time capsules

Each pupil downloads one .json file. It contains a codename, dates, the prediction, cone position, confidence, reasoning, signals, pitch summary and a question for their future self. The page refuses to seal if it spots an email address or UK phone number.

Storing them. Collect the files into a folder on the school system (Glow, OneDrive or Teams). The school is the data controller; nothing is stored online by the site.

Passphrase. If you give the class a passphrase, files are encrypted in the browser (AES-GCM, key from PBKDF2). The codename and dates stay readable so you can sort files. Write the passphrase down somewhere safe: without it, the files can't be opened.

Opening. Pupils can open their own capsule on the unit's page from the opening date. You can open any capsule at any time with the tool here, and export a class table.

Answer notes

Most activities give instant feedback. These are the ones worth discussing.

4.3 Quotes

Fake or no evidence: Watson "five computers", Gates "640K", Star Trek inspired the mobile phone. Out of context: Olsen 1977. Real: Ballmer 2007, Metcalfe 1995, Stoll 1995.

3.5 Data cleaning

8 problems: two unit mix-ups (RAM in MB and GB), "48K", a duplicate PS2 row, the 2070 typo, a blank PS5 value, and two source conflicts (AGC 2.048 vs 1.024 MHz; BBC Micro £335 vs £399). The point: find out why sources disagree, don't just pick one.

4.2 Prediction lab

Clarke 1964 and Asimov's robots accept Hit or Partly. Sinclair C5 is "Partly": a flop in 1985, but e-scooters and e-bikes made the idea mainstream.

8.2 Moore's Law guess

Hidden 2024 points: Apple M4 (28 billion) and Nvidia B200 (208 billion). A guess within 10× of 208 billion earns the stamp.
