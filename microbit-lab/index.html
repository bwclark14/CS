How Bit Bench works

    
    
      
  1. Build. Add parts from the Parts list. Each part is wired to a micro:bit pin. Click a part to change which pin it uses.
    2. 
      
  2. Check the wiring. The Wiring check tab spots real-world problems: pins you can't reach without a breakout board, two parts on one pin, pins the LED display or buttons already use, and motors without a driver board.
    3. 
      
  3. Code. Snap blocks together. Pin blocks talk to your parts, just like MakeCode.
    4. 
      
  4. Run. Press Run. Click buttons A and B and the gold logo on the micro:bit, press button parts, and move the sliders in the Inputs tab to change what the sensors read.
    5. 
      
  5. Drive. Add a motor driver board and two motors (M1 left, M2 right) and your build becomes a rover. Open the Rover arena to drive it. Sonar and line sensors read the arena.
    6. 
      
  6. Take it to real hardware. The MicroPython tab shows the same program for a real micro:bit.
